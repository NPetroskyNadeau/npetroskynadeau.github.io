Young Adults Are Losing Their Job-Finding Edge

Nicolas Petrosky-Nadeau · November 3, 2026

Download the data (CSV)

People in their twenties have long moved from outside the labor market into a job more frequently than workers in mid-career. That is changing, and the gap is the narrowest in three decades of data.

The teal line follows people out of the labor force (neither working nor looking, as many students and new graduates are) and their chance of starting a job in a given month. In 2022, as before the pandemic, a 21-to-30-year-old in that position was about 56% more likely to make the move than a 35-to-54-year-old. By August 2026 the difference was down to 31%, as young adults’ monthly rate slipped from about 12% to 10% while experienced workers’ remained unchanged.

This is one more signal that the first step into a career has become harder.
