Wage Growth Has Tilted Toward Experience

Nicolas Petrosky-Nadeau · October 30, 2026

Download the data (CSV)

Pay grows fastest early in a career, so 21-to-30-year-olds normally see faster wage growth than 35-to-54-year-olds. In 2016–2019 the gap was about 3 percentage points. By August 2026 it was down to 1.8 (teal), closer to the slack labor markets of 2003–2005 and 2009–2014, and well below the 2.9 that today’s unemployment rate would imply on its 1994–2019 relationship with the gap (gold).

Most of the narrowing, about 60%, is on the mid-career side: median wage growth for 35-to-54-year-olds averaged 3.8% in 2025–2026, up from 3.1% in 2015–2019, while the young slowed from 6.0% to 5.6%. In 2026 alone, though, the slowdown among the young has been the larger part.

Measured against the slow pace of hiring instead, the gap is about where the cycle would put it, at 1.9 points. A labor market that hires slowly and rarely lays off appears to favor those with experience.
