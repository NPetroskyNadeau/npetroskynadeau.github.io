Nicolas Petrosky-Nadeau

Vice President for Macroeconomic Research

Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco

I lead the macroeconomic research group at the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, where I advise the Bank President on monetary policy for FOMC meetings. My research focuses on labor market dynamics using search and matching models, with publications in leading journals.

I serve on the policy advisory group for the SF Fed President, where I co-coordinate research briefings and consult on monetary policy strategies for FOMC meetings. I also manage the SF Fed Data Explorer, a public interactive data application, and organize the annual Micro-Macro Labor Economics Conference.

My research uses search and matching models to examine fluctuations in unemployment, job vacancies, and wages, incorporating product and credit market frictions. I have published in the American Economic Review, the Journal of Monetary Economics, the Journal of Economic Theory, and the Journal of Political Economy: Macroeconomics, among others, and co-authored a graduate textbook with MIT Press. I have served as Associate Editor of the European Economic Review and was a National Fellow at the Hoover Institution at Stanford.

Prior to joining the Federal Reserve in 2014, I was on the faculty at Carnegie Mellon University's Tepper School of Business. I hold a PhD in economics from the University of Quebec at Montreal and Sciences Po Paris, and an M.A. from the Graduate Institute in Geneva.

Research Interests

Macroeconomic labor markets, search and matching models, unemployment dynamics, job vacancies, wage determination, business cycle fluctuations, product market frictions, credit market frictions, monetary policy implications
